# Report bibliography and evidence notes
Prepared 13 October 2026. Citation IDs match the twenty-page report. Individual researcher sources and activity evidence are recorded separately in the directory.

## Official statistics and background directory

**R1.** U.S. Census Bureau. *QuickFacts: California*. Accessed 13 October 2026. The displayed Hispanic/Latino percentage was 41.2%; median gross rent for 2020–2024 was $2,036. QuickFacts combines indicators with different reference years; the population percentage is not an age-matched faculty denominator. https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/CA

**R2.** National Center for Science and Engineering Statistics, National Science Foundation. *Survey of Earned Doctorates 2024*, NSF 25-349, Table 3-3: “Research doctorate recipients, by detailed field of doctorate, citizenship status, ethnicity, and race: 2024.” For neurobiology/neurosciences: 1,238 total recipients; 978 U.S. citizens/permanent residents; 127 Hispanic/Latino domestic recipients. The 13.0% calculation uses 127/978, not the total international-plus-domestic count. https://ncses.nsf.gov/pubs/nsf25349/assets/data-tables/tables/nsf25349-tab003-003.pdf

**R3.** Fred Hutch Cancer Center. *Atlas of Inspiring Hispanic/Latinx Scientists*. Publicly curated faculty directory; page dated 29 June 2026 in the examined source. Used as public background recognition, not as proof of nationality or verbatim personal self-identification. The directory itself is not exhaustive. https://www.fredhutch.org/en/about/about-the-hutch/faculty-development/atlas-of-inspiring-hispanic-latinx-scientists.html

## Peer-reviewed access, training and academic-career research

**R4.** Rendon, M. G., Flores, G. M., & Schaefer, D. R. (2025). Navigating the Culture of Biology: Comparing Latinas’ Experience with Asian and Black Undergraduate Women at an MSI. *CBE—Life Sciences Education*, 24(4), ar50. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.25-05-0101

Evidence: full text examined through Europe PMC, PMC12667361. Qualitative California UC-campus study; 35 women, including 21 self-identified Latinas, eight Asian participants and six African American participants. Institution anonymized by the authors. Not a statewide prevalence study.

**R5.** Estrada, M., Hernandez, P. R., & Schultz, P. W. (2018). A Longitudinal Study of How Quality Mentorship and Research Experience Integrate Underrepresented Minorities into STEM Careers. *CBE—Life Sciences Education*, 17(1), ar9. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.17-04-0066

Evidence: full text examined, PMC6007776. Analytic sample N = 1,015; 40.3% Hispanic/Latino/Latina. Longitudinal associations; not randomized mentoring assignment.

**R6.** Estrada, M., et al. (2016). Improving Underrepresented Minority Student Persistence in STEM. *CBE—Life Sciences Education*, 15(3), es5. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.16-01-0038

Evidence: full text examined, PMC5008901. Review and recommendations emphasizing institutional accountability; not a California neuroscience intervention trial.

**R7.** Morgan, A. C., LaBerge, N., Larremore, D. B., Galesic, M., Brand, J. E., & Clauset, A. (2022). Socioeconomic roots of academic faculty. *Nature Human Behaviour*, 6(12), 1625–1633. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-022-01425-4

Evidence: full text examined, PMC9755046. Survey of 7,204 tenure-track faculty across eight fields; 22.2% reported a parent with a PhD. Not Latino-specific or California neuroscience-specific.

**R8.** Hofstra, B., Kulkarni, V. V., Munoz-Najar Galvez, S., He, B., Jurafsky, D., & McFarland, D. A. (2020). The Diversity–Innovation Paradox in Science. *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*, 117(17), 9284–9291. https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1915378117

Evidence: metadata and abstract examined. Large-scale observational/computational study of approximately 1.2 million U.S. doctoral recipients, 1977–2015. The measures are not a comprehensive definition of scientific quality and are not California Latino neuroscience estimates.

**R9.** Sealy, L., Saunders, C., Blume, J., & Chalkley, R. (2019). The GRE over the entire range of scores lacks predictive ability for PhD outcomes in the biomedical sciences. *PLOS ONE*, 14(3), e0201634. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0201634

Evidence: metadata and abstract examined. Vanderbilt IMSD cohort of 32 students; 28 completed the PhD at reporting. Small, single-program study; not proof that all selection criteria are nonpredictive.

**R10.** Chen, C. Y., Kahanamoku, S. S., Tripati, A., Alegado, R. A., Morris, V. R., Andrade, K., & Hosbey, J. (2022). Systemic racial disparities in funding rates at the National Science Foundation. *eLife*, 11, e83071. https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.83071

Evidence: full text examined, PMC9708090. NSF administrative data, 1996–2019; more than one million decisions. Hispanic ethnicity and race tabulation changed over time. Descriptive disparities do not independently identify a particular causal mechanism or provide a current California estimate.

**R11.** Ginther, D. K., Schaffer, W. T., Schnell, J., Masimore, B., Liu, F., Haak, L. L., & Kington, R. (2011). Race, ethnicity, and NIH research awards. *Science*, 333(6045), 1015–1019. https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1196783

Evidence: metadata and abstract examined. The adjusted ten-percentage-point disadvantage commonly quoted from this study concerns Black applicants relative to White applicants; it is not a ten-point Latino funding deficit.

**R12.** Maldonado-Vlaar, C. S., & García-Arrarás, J. E. (2024). The Neuroscience Research Opportunities to Increase Diversity Program: Transformative and Successful Research Training Strategies for Undergraduate Students within Hispanic Serving Institutions. *Journal of Undergraduate Neuroscience Education*, 22(3), A224–A232. https://doi.org/10.59390/zglz3652

Evidence: metadata and abstract examined. Program account from UPR–Río Piedras; not a randomized California outcome comparison. The report uses the described program structure and reported direction toward graduate training, not an invented placement percentage.

**R13.** Theobald, E. J., et al. (2020). Active learning narrows achievement gaps for underrepresented students in undergraduate science, technology, engineering, and math. *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*, 117(12), 6476–6483. https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1916903117

Evidence: metadata and abstract examined. Meta-analysis: examination data from 15 studies/9,238 students; failure-rate data from 26 studies/44,606 students. Average gap reductions of 33% and 45%, respectively; not California Latino neuroscience-specific effects.

**R14.** Lopatto, D. (2007). Undergraduate research experiences support science career decisions and active learning. *CBE—Life Sciences Education*, 6(4), 297–306. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.07-06-0039

Evidence: metadata and abstract examined. Student-reported outcomes from research experiences; not a randomized estimate of later PI attainment.

**R15.** Morgan, A. C., Way, S. F., Hoefer, M. J. D., Larremore, D. B., Galesic, M., & Clauset, A. (2021). The unequal impact of parenthood in academia. *Science Advances*, 7(9), eabd1996. https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.abd1996

Evidence: metadata and abstract examined. Survey of 3,064 tenure-track faculty at 450 computer-science, history and business departments in the U.S. and Canada. Used as broader care-policy evidence, not a Latino neuroscience study.

## Search and access limitations

The review used direct institutional and laboratory pages, web-search results, official datasets, public biographies and Europe PMC records. The named research plugins were unavailable as callable tools and were not used. Some publisher/profile pages returned access errors or generic redirects. Search-engine rate limits curtailed later query expansion. No CAPTCHA was bypassed. Five selected peer-reviewed full texts were examined successfully: R4, R5, R6, R7 and R10. Other cited papers were evaluated through metadata/abstracts, as stated above. No interview, private demographic search, or consent-based statewide census was conducted.
