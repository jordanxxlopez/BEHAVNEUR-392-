# Source and teaching review

Scope: Carlson and Birkett (2022), Chapter 19, printed pages 604–635, uploaded Part6of6 PDF pages 84–115. The sequence covers reinforcement, cues and relapse, vulnerability, opioids, stimulants, nicotine, alcohol, cannabis, and treatments.

There are 31 foundation slides and 47 research slides. Primary-source metadata, relevant results, original figure legends, and movie preparations were checked against eLife version-of-record data and accessible publisher/JATS full text. PAPERS.md records the primary sources.

Specific distinctions retained:

- Positive reinforcement, negative reinforcement, punishment, tolerance, physical dependence, and substance use disorder have separate definitions.
- The odor-learning dopamine projection terminates in medial olfactory tubercle, not nucleus accumbens.
- Visual dopamine responses, psychomotor sensitization, incentive-related anticipatory behavior, and subjective pleasure are not treated as one measurement.
- Mu- and delta-receptor trafficking experiments use their actual receptors and preparations. Golgi delta-receptor signaling is not presented as footage of maintenance treatment.
- The first receptor-labeling depth series uses an FMOR mouse with a tagged receptor. The later rat preparation demonstrates native receptors without requiring a genetically tagged receptor.
- The cocaine calcium movies are a baseline and the same field after acute application. Drug seeking was measured separately in animals.
- The fluorescent drug sensor and its structural movie are not nicotinic receptor channels. ER and plasma-membrane fluorescence measure compartmental access, not clinical efficacy.
- Worm nicotine and ethanol experiments are explicit sensory-motor or channel assays, not complete human addiction models.
- CB1 locomotor movies are not mislabeled as eyelid-conditioning sessions. Global receptor deletion can alter the state in which learning is tested.

Legacy treatment claims are corrected: buprenorphine/naloxone is not described as having zero misuse potential; naloxone rescue differs from maintenance; acamprosate's complete therapeutic mechanism remains under study; CBD is not a guaranteed antipsychotic treatment or simply a universal CB1 antagonist. Clinical diagnosis and treatment language remains non-stigmatizing while the assigned chapter title is preserved.

Original scientific imagery is retained, including the publisher's own sensor and molecular visualizations. The CB1 somatodendritic recording is an explicitly identified callback to a previously taught cellular mechanism. The worm receptor study also serves a new receptor-specific teaching purpose. Forty-seven cached Neuroethology source catalogs showed no selected DOI overlap. No repeated excerpt is counted as a separate recording.
