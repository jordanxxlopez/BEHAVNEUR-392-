# Source and teaching review

Scope: Carlson and Birkett (2022), Chapter 18, printed pages 580–603, uploaded Part6of6 PDF pages 60–83. The chapter includes stress physiology, prolonged and developmental exposure, immune regulation, PTSD, anxiety disorders, OCD, and treatments.

The teaching sequence interweaves 31 foundation slides with 47 research slides. Primary-source metadata, relevant results, figure legends, and recording preparations were checked against original eLife version-of-record data and accessible publisher/JATS full text. The core studies are listed in PAPERS.md.

Specific distinctions retained:

- CRH-cell activation, endocrine output, and context-dependent behavior are different measurements.
- Spinal norepinephrine effects depend on projection, cellular target, and pain preparation.
- Inflammatory neutrophil imaging is explicitly identified as a cellular preparation, not a psychological-stress experiment.
- VR context recall, danger-cue ethograms, extinction, and avoidance tasks show distinct defensive actions.
- The indirect-pathway imaging result differs from the simple prediction that direct-pathway hyperactivity explains all compulsive grooming.
- The four striatal calcium movies are baseline imaging preparations. They are not labeled as footage of fluoxetine treatment or optical inhibition.
- Human app training distinguishes subjective habit reports, objective automaticity, and later effort-based preferences.

Legacy claims are not copied uncritically: B cells produce antibodies; SSRIs inhibit reuptake rather than directly activating serotonin receptors; basal-ganglia projection neurons release inhibitory transmitter; a mouse grooming measure does not establish human obsessions. Clinical outcomes remain separate from animal and cell measurements.

Original scientific figures and publisher recordings are preserved. Textbook crops retain original labels and have exact MediaBox crop coordinates. A few figures from other assigned book chapters provide directly matching anatomy; their actual figure numbers and pages remain credited. No fabricated or redrawn scientific imagery is used.

Forty-seven cached Neuroethology catalogs were checked for selected DOI overlap; none was found. Two grooming examples from the same classifier paper serve distinct purposes: defining ordinary grooming and evaluating recognition across visual backgrounds. They are not presented as different studies or as coat-color effects on OCD.
