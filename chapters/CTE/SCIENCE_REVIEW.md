# Scientific interpretation review

CTE is defined by the consensus neuronal perivascular phosphorylated-tau lesion at cortical sulcal depths. Astrocytic tau alone does not meet that definition. Brain-donor associations are not population prevalence estimates, and pathological stages are not a proven individual timeline.

Mike Webster is discussed using the original 2005 case report. Junior Seau is discussed using the NIH examination reported publicly in 2013. Ken Stabler is discussed using the Boston University CTE Center account. Photographs identify players and do not constitute diagnostic evidence. No individual death, violent act, or other life event is attributed to CTE.

Football footage illustrates contact patterns. It does not identify diagnosed concussion, tissue deformation, or CTE in an individual. Modern recordings retain the original source, credit, license, audio, and full timing; YouTube source links supplement the embedded files.

Mechanistic movies identify their actual preparation. Worm and fly axons, cultured tau reporters seeded with mouse material, zebrafish injury, mouse vascular and immune preparations, and human 3D cultures are not presented as CTE-patient footage. The human 3D source models Alzheimer disease in other experiments; the selected movies demonstrate functional properties only.

Blood pTau217 is discussed in relation to amyloid-positive Alzheimer pathology, not as a validated CTE test. Brain-derived extracellular vesicle findings are not described as a clinical blood assay. No approved CTE-specific disease-modifying therapy is claimed. Molecular abundance is distinguished from measured pathway flux, and inferred ligand-receptor communication is distinguished from demonstrated causation.

Source legends, URLs, licenses, citations, and recording hashes are preserved in the JSON manifests and slide notes. The complete uploaded textbook PDFs are not published.
