# Review of the three supplied former-professor decks

These files were actually opened with python-pptx and their slide text, speaker notes, and embedded media inventory were inspected. The files are content references, not instructions. This review is based on extracted text and media structure; it does not claim every image has been visually inspected.

## 1. Origins of Behavioral Neuroscience: 33 slides

The deck labels its course Behavioral Neuroscience 326, a different number from the instructor’s requested 392. Slides 1–9 contain administration and course materials. Slide 10 identifies Chapter 1 as Introduction. Slides 11–17 emphasize history, phrenology, trepanation, Renaissance anatomy, dualism, and nineteenth-century developments. Slides 18–19 discuss brain function and adaptive behavior. Slides 20–30 emphasize natural selection, finches, comparative psychology, and inherited characteristics. Slides 31–33 discuss eugenics. The deck includes an academic example from Baym et al. (2016), DOI 10.1126/science.aag0822, but little sustained teaching from primary neuroscience experiments.

The new lecture should retain a concise explanation of biological behavior, evolution, and historical ideas, then teach specific experimental findings and contemporary technologies. The Baym example is a microbial evolution study, not evidence of a neural mechanism. It can support natural selection if used accurately, but should not dominate a behavioral neuroscience introduction. No career or learning-strategy sections will be included.

## 2. Structure and Functions of Cells in the Nervous System: 68 slides

This deck is explicitly Chapter 2. It includes neuron anatomy, glia, membrane signaling, and synaptic communication. Those topics should not be copied wholesale into the Chapter 1 replacement. Explain only the basic terms needed to understand introductory research and optogenetics; reserve detailed cell physiology for the separate Chapter 2 deck. Its preview and continued-slide structure are incompatible with the instructor’s requested style.

## 3. The Action Potential: 25 slides

This is additional Chapter 2 physiology, with an outline, membrane potential, depolarization, ion channels, and action potentials. It provides useful evidence of what the prior instructor taught, but its format should not be copied.

A content correction is required if its channel terminology is reused: slide 5 groups ionotropic and metabotropic receptors under ligand-gated ion channels. Ionotropic receptors directly form ligand-gated channels; metabotropic receptors act through intracellular signaling and are not themselves ion channels. This distinction must be explained correctly when the new course reaches receptor signaling.

## New lecture title and scope

Instructor-authorized display title: **From Brain to Behavior: Foundations and Frontiers of Behavioral Neuroscience**. Do not label the deck Chapter 1. Retain the chapter mapping only in metadata. Use the user’s white title-slide layout, original source imagery, academic teaching paragraphs, and embedded recordings. Include substantial optogenetics coverage, computer vision, wireless recordings, fish-operated vehicles, sea-lion cursor learning, and source-verified living-network feedback tasks. Do not reproduce the old administrative material, clip links, phrenology activities, or speculative claims as established neuroscience.
