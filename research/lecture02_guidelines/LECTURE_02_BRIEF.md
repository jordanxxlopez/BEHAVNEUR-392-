## Applicable requirements for the second lecture

### Course, scope, and count

- Nineteen chapter decks based on Carlson and Birkett, Physiology of Behavior, Global Edition, 13th edition (2022), ISBN 9781292430256.
- Verify Chapter 2 directly against the correct edition before declaring the chapter title, page range, or scope verified. Use a descriptive instructor-authorized display title, not “Chapter 2.” Do not invent meeting dates or a timetable.
- Undergraduate level, easier than NEUR 411 but intellectually demanding. Define unfamiliar terms when first used and explain mechanisms in concrete steps.
- Target 75 slides: title, 73 content, final Key takeaways. Expand only when useful, to 85 maximum. The 85–90-slide permission belongs to the Chapter 1 history expansion only.
- Review the authoritative Chapter 1 history-revision spec before allocating content, since its expanded history and research sections overlap potential introductory cellular material.

### Source balance and sequencing

- 30–40% genuinely taught foundations and 60–70% genuinely taught primary research; tag each content slide instructional_role. At 73 content slides, 29 foundation and 44 research slides give the preferred balance.
- Interweave a concept with directly relevant studies rather than putting all foundations first. An incidental citation does not make a slide research teaching.
- Use a manageable core set of papers over several slides, combining current accessible work with essential classic studies.
- Explain preparation, manipulation, measured behavior, neuronal mechanism, and necessary experimental conditions where relevant; do not impose a repeated method/result/limitation template.
- Every factual and quantitative claim must be supported by a source actually reviewed. Do not transfer unsupported assertions from a prior deck or status report.
- Do not say “the textbook” in visible teaching prose; identify the book accurately in source credits and references.

### Writing and notes

- Three substantive teaching paragraphs on every content slide. Each paragraph must occupy 5–7 actual rendered lines in the exported PDF. This overrides inherited sentence counts and earlier 70–110-word targets.
- Direct, natural teaching sentences identify the actor, action, and outcome. Explain signaling, channels, receptors, neurotransmitters, pathways, and behavior step by step.
- Avoid vague summaries with “linked,” “connected,” “supported,” or “relationship”; describe the actual action or anatomy.
- No filler, roadmap, agendas, objectives, previews, transition announcements, figure-reading narration, evaluative framing, “continued” splits, or em dashes in visible text.
- No routine limitation endings. Retain scientifically necessary qualifications and label hypotheses as hypotheses in the relevant sentence.
- No p-values, named statistical tests, uncertainty bookkeeping, or sample-size bookkeeping. Retain quantities that explain the scientific concept.
- Natural complete-sentence teaching transcripts in speaker notes, organized in readable bullets/sub-bullets where useful, followed by References and full DOI citations.
- Do not invent equations, calculations, worked examples, or data beyond the sources.
- Short claim-style content titles, normally no more than 62 characters without a final period, consistent with the inherited format.
- End with 5–6 exam-level factual takeaways, each with a bold lead phrase.
