# Teaching prose and speaker transcripts

This instructor correction applies to all Behavioral Neuroscience chapters and authorized revisions. It applies equally to visible slide text and the teaching transcript before References in speaker notes. Source credits and original figure legends retain their source wording.

Teach the mechanism, anatomy, physiology, and behavior directly, at the plain-language level of the instructor's supplied cannabinoid rewrite below. Use connected explanations, definitions woven into the prose, and clear descriptions of what changes in the cell. A slide should leave the student able to explain a specific biological process, not merely repeat that a study examined it. Do not replace vague prose with a dense list of technical proteins or experimental abbreviations.

- Name the actor and explain its action. For example, myosin uses ATP to generate force against actin; it does not merely "recruit cellular machinery."
- Define a technical term where students first need it. An enzyme is not explained by naming several other enzymes. Introduce the relevant structure, action, and consequence in ordinary language.
- Explain intermediate steps when the sources establish them. State where a compound enters, which receptor it reaches, what the receptor changes, and how the measured response changes. Do not fill a gap in the evidence with an invented pathway.
- Use source-specific findings: which cell or animal, what treatment or manipulation, what changed, and in which direction. Explain an inhibitor by naming the step it blocks and the response that persists or disappears.
- Each paragraph adds information. Paragraph three may teach a further mechanism, a different condition, a follow-up result, or a relevant anatomical or behavioral detail. It has no fixed closing function.
- Retain a conclusion when it makes a concrete biological claim supported by the preceding evidence. Delete conclusions that merely praise the experiment, announce a connection, or say that several factors matter.
- Write connected sentences that sound natural aloud. Avoid both dense strings of unexplained terms and choppy lists of obvious facts. Do not add a fixed number of sentences.
- Preserve scientifically necessary distinctions, including hypothesis versus result, cultured cells versus intact animals, and calcium fluorescence versus transmitter release. State the distinction within the explanation rather than adding a stock caution paragraph.

Apply this deletion test to every sentence: would its removal lose a definition, mechanism, specific finding, experimental detail, or necessary scientific distinction? If not, delete or replace it. Apply the test separately to every final paragraph. Automated wording checks catch recurring failures; they do not assess scientific depth.

## Rejected wording

"The study brings drug action back to a complete causal sequence: a compound reaches a receptor, the receptor recruits intracellular signaling, effectors alter cellular function, and the changed cell contributes to a larger neural system."

This does not explain the CB1 signaling pathway, force generation, dendritic response, or the inhibitor results. Other unacceptable substitutes include "the findings place the mechanism in a broader context," "the experiment highlights the importance of cellular machinery," and "access and target location all matter."

## Instructor's preferred example

Cannabinoids can change the shape of neurons by activating CB1 receptors, which trigger signals inside the cell that affect its structure. In this study, researchers examined hippocampal neurons grown in a laboratory and observed changes in the cell body and dendrites, the branches that receive signals from other neurons. Together, these structures make up the somatodendritic region, so the findings showed that cannabinoid effects extended beyond the growing tip of the axon to other parts of the neuron.

These structural changes involve actin, a protein that forms part of the cytoskeleton, which is the internal framework that supports the cell and helps it change shape. When CB1 receptors are activated, signals inside the neuron can change how actin is organized, allowing the cell body and dendrites to change their shape. The images follow the same neuron over several minutes, allowing us to compare its appearance before and after receptor activation and see how its structure changes over time.

The length of receptor activation is important because changes in shape that happen within minutes are different from changes in growth or organization that develop after longer exposure. Researchers examined these effects separately to distinguish the neuron's immediate response from changes that emerge over time. This study shows how a chemical acting on a receptor can produce a physical change in a neuron by triggering signals that reorganize its internal framework and alter the shape of its cell body and dendrites.

Source: Roland et al. (2014), Figure 7. https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.03159.

This example is the instructor's style reference, not a requirement to use these exact sentence lengths or a template for every slide. Maintain three paragraphs and the course's rendered-line requirement. Meaningful references to a recorded change or a concrete biological conclusion are allowed; empty statements about a study's importance are not.
